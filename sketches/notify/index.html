Notifications, state by state

A notification is a card in the bottom right corner of the surface, saying something happened that nobody was looking at. Nothing here runs. It is drawn before it is built, so that it is argued about once.

The corner

#corner

Bottom right of the surface the library was given, not of the browser window: the library does not draw outside its surface, and a host whose dock is half the page wants its notes over its dock. It is drawn over whatever window is there and moves nothing, because a layout that jumps when a message arrives is a layout nobody can work in.

The card

#card

Rounded at --radius-lg, the one shape on the surface that is not a tile: everything else meets its neighbours edge to edge, and a rounded card is how it says it is not one of them. A firm border and a soft shadow lift it off the window beneath; the shadow alone disappears on white.

A card can carry actions, as buttons under its line. Pressing one is an action the machine emits, naming the card and the button, and the host does the rest; the card then leaves, because what it said has been answered.

More than one

#stack

The newest is nearest the corner and the older ones are pushed up, 8px apart. The corner is where the eye goes when something arrives, so the thing that just arrived is the thing there.

Warnings and errors

#kinds

Three kinds: something happened, something might be wrong, and something went wrong. Only the mark carries the kind — amber for a warning, and red kept for the one thing red is for. A coloured card is an alarm, and neither of these is always one.

Coming and going

#coming

A card slides up from the bottom edge and fades in, over --fade. The ones above it move up to make room over the same time, so the whole stack rises together and nothing jumps.

It leaves on its own after five seconds, or when dismissed, by fading in place; the ones above it then settle down into the room it left. The five seconds are a constant in dock-state, and the time arrives with the frame's batch of events, so the machine still has no clock of its own.
